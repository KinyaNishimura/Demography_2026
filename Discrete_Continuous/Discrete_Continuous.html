▶︎ At the level of observation, "being alive" is an event that occurred

When we observe at each interval, what is recorded at each observation is either "alive" or "dead." Seen purely at the level of observation, the fact that "being alive was observed at this observation" is an event that actually occurred and was recorded, with the same standing as the observation of "death."

▶︎ An observation of "being alive" extends into the past

There is an important point about observing "being alive." Because death, once it has occurred, cannot be undone, a single observation that "the individual was alive at time t" tells us the following at the same time:

  • The individual was alive at time t.

  • The individual was alive at every earlier time as well.

In other words, an observation of "being alive" confirms not only that moment but the whole period of life from birth to that moment.

On the other hand, an observation of "death" tells us only two things:

  • The individual was already dead at time t.

  • Death occurred sometime after the last time "being alive" was observed and before t.

Thus, although both the observation of "being alive" and the observation of "death" are events at the level of observation, they differ in how far the information they give extends.

▶︎ Life and death: the layer of observation and the layer of phenomena

"Being alive" and "death" are viewed differently depending on whether we consider the observation of the phenomenon or the phenomenon itself.

  • The layer of observation: the results recorded at each observation. Both "being alive" and "death" are events that occurred.

  • The layer of phenomena: what happens to the organism itself over time. The only events that occur at an instant are birth and death. "Being alive" is a state that continues from birth until death.

When we observe in discrete time, "being alive" and "death" stand side by side as equal events at the layer of observation.

✔️ Interval width 1 and interval width Δx

For interval width 1,

sx+qx=1,

so sx and qx are both probabilities, and they are complementary: they sum to 1. In contrast, for interval width Δx,

(5)sx|Δx+qx|ΔxΔx=1,

so sx|Δx and qx|Δx do not sum to 1 as they are.

Because qx|Δx was defined as a "rate per interval width," that is, (lx−lx+Δx)/lxΔx, the probability of dying in the interval [x,x+Δx) is qx|ΔxΔx. On the other hand, sx|Δx is defined as lx+Δxlx, so it is, without division by the interval width, the probability itself of surviving the interval [x,x+Δx). sx|Δx is a probability and qx|Δx is a rate; the two probabilities that are complementary are sx|Δx and qx|ΔxΔx.

▶︎ The layer of observation and the layer of phenomena

When the observation interval is taken as Δx→0, the aspect of the layer of phenomena is emphasized more than that of the layer of observation. When time is made continuous and only the layer of phenomena is considered, the only event that occurs at an instant is death, and survival is expressed, as "death not having occurred yet," solely by the likelihood of death.

Below, from the survival function in discrete time, we derive the survival function in continuous time, in order to focus more on the layer of phenomena.

 

Take the logarithm of both sides of Eq. (4):

ln⁡lX=ln⁡∏k=0n−1(1−qxk|ΔxΔx)(6)=∑k=0n−1ln⁡(1−qxk|ΔxΔx).

In Eq. (6), we omit "|Δx" and write qxk|Δx as qxk:

(7)ln⁡lX=∑k=0n−1ln⁡(1−qxkΔx).

Applying the Maclaurin expansion of the function ln⁡(1−y) of y,

ln⁡(1−y)=−y−y22−y33−⋯,

to each term on the right-hand side of Eq. (7) and substituting y=qxkΔx, we obtain

ln⁡(1−qxkΔx)=−qxkΔx−qxk22(Δx)2−qxk33(Δx)3−⋯.

When the interval width Δx is sufficiently small and the probability of dying in the interval, y=qxkΔx, is small ( 0≤y≤12), the following inequality holds:

−y−y2≤ln⁡(1−y)≤−y.

Setting y=qxkΔx and summing as in Eq. (7), we obtain the following relationship:

−∑k=0n−1qxkΔx−∑k=0n−1qxk2(Δx)2≤ln⁡lX≤−∑k=0n−1qxkΔx.

Each term of the remainder ∑k=0n−1qxk2(Δx)2 is proportional to (Δx)2, so even when multiplied by the number of terms n=X/Δx, the whole remains of a size proportional to Δx, and it goes to 0 as Δx→0. On the other hand, ∑k=0n−1qxkΔx does not go to 0 but approaches a finite value. Therefore, we obtain the relationship

ln⁡lX=−limΔx→0∑k=0n−1qxkΔx.

Here, we again write qxk as qxk|Δx.

As Δx→0, we consider a continuous function μ(x) such that limΔx→0qxk|Δx=μ(xk).

 

As Δx→0, the sum becomes an integral as the limit of a Riemann sum:

ln⁡lX⟶−∫0Xμ(t)dt.

The lX on the left-hand side is the value at the same age X for every n. Therefore, this limit gives an equation for "the survival probability at age X":

l(X)=exp(−∫0Xμ(t)dt).

Since X can be chosen arbitrarily, this determines the function l on [0,∞). Only at this point can l be written as a function of continuous age.

 

When Δx is finite, the survival function is

lX=∏k=0n−1(1−qxk|ΔxΔx)=∏k=0n−1sxk|Δx.

It could be expressed using either the survival or the death parameters, but as Δx→0, it comes to be expressed only by the function μ(x) such that limΔx→0qxk|Δx=μ(xk).

The function μ(x) in the continuous-time model, which corresponds to qx or qxk|Δx (the period death rate) in the discrete-time model, is called the "instantaneous death rate," reflecting the meaning of Δx→0.
